# Task 2 submission form - Kestrel Home service-request routing (Variant B)

## 1. What did you build, and what business decision does it support? State the number and the rupees.
The old labels are not the truth: team_label is the queue the old bot chose, and in 23% of cases (2,471 of 10,822) the request ended up with a different team, so matching those labels at 90% would copy the bot's mistakes. Roughly a fifth of its errors are "customer says they paid" sent to Billing and another fifth are purifier breakdowns sent to Consumables. I built a text router (FastAPI endpoint + one screen, runs offline, no API key) trained on where requests actually ended up (resolution_log.final_team). It supports Ritu's decision to retire the Rs 3.2 lakh/yr bot, but with a different success test. Number: 84% right on months it never saw, vs 77% for the bot; 97% right on the ~80% of requests it is confident about, with the other ~20% flagged for a person. Rupees: today about Rs 18.4 lakh/yr (licence + transfers + repeat contacts); with the router about Rs 6.9 lakh/yr counting each human check at the policy's Rs 305; saving about Rs 11.5 lakh/yr (about Rs 14 lakh if a check costs Rs 150). AI charges: Rs 0.

## 2. What score do you expect predictions.csv to get on the hidden outcomes, on which metric, and why that metric? Say how you estimated it.
About 84% accuracy (likely range 82-86%), macro-F1 about 0.84, measured against the team that finally closed each request. Accuracy because it is Ritu's own "match" test and each wrong routing costs about Rs 685 in the policy's terms; I also checked macro-F1 so a weak team cannot hide. Estimated by training only on the past and testing on the next three months, twice: Jan-Mar 2026 (2,168 requests) 84.5%, Apr-Jun 2026 (2,135 requests) 83.7%. The final model is trained on all 10,822 rows. The test file covers Jul-Sep 2026; every word in it appears in training and the product mix is unchanged, so I do not expect a big drift. If the hidden score is against the bot's queue instead of real outcomes, expect it to be lower, about 80%.

## 3. How do you know it works? How you validated, on what split, error rate, and the kind of case it gets wrong.
Time-based split, never random, because the test set is the most recent requests: train on everything before a cutoff, test on the following three months, twice (84.5% and 83.7%; the bot scored 77.5% and 76.7% on the same windows). Error rate about 16%. Per-team recall is 78-91% (lowest Product Advice 78%, highest Repairs 91%). What it gets wrong: vague messages with no clue ("please call me about my purifier"), which it tends to send to Repairs; Repairs precision is only 76% for that reason. At a 70% confidence threshold it auto-routes 80% of requests at 96.6% accuracy and flags the rest. Full tables and confusion matrix: reports/evidence.md in the repo; 6 automated tests pass on the service.

## 4. Did you change, narrow, or push back on the client's ask? What, when, and why.
Yes, before building anything, right after comparing team_label with resolution_log. Ritu asked for a model that matches the labels at 90%. I changed the target: the model is trained on where each request actually ended up, not on the bot's queue, because the labels agree with the final team only 77% of the time (a model trained to copy them scores about 96% on them and about 76% on real outcomes, so it passes the test and is still wrong). I narrowed the promise: 90% on every request is not reachable from the message text, so the router auto-routes the confident 80% (96.6% right) and flags the rest for a person. I also recommended counting headcount by the team that finally closes requests, because the bot's queue overstates Repairs (29% vs 23% real).

## 5. What is wrong with what you are handing us, or with the data we handed you?
Data: product_family disagrees with the product named in the text in 17.5% of rows, so I do not use it; transfers contradicts first/final team in 477 rows (351 show transfers but the same team, 126 show none but different teams); legacy Zoho resolved_at is in UTC, so 26% of legacy requests "resolve" before they were created; 11% of legacy texts have broken characters (cleaned); two team names changed on 15 Jan 2026 (merged for training); 92 identical text+product combinations ended in different teams, so some requests cannot be routed from text; train labels are the bot's queue. What I hand over: vague messages are often wrong; it over-predicts Repairs (614 of 2,178 test rows = 28% vs about 23% real), so do not use these predictions to plan headcount; the "reasons" are the model's strongest words, not proof; the Rs 150 human-check cost is my assumption (the headline uses the policy's Rs 305); it has not seen July-Sep outcomes.

## 6. What did you deliberately leave out, and why that rather than something else?
No LLM or model API, and I did not test one, so I have no measured number for it: about 41% of the bot's errors are vague messages that do not contain enough information for any model, and a paid API would add exactly the running cost Finance said it does not want. No product, warranty or channel columns: they did not improve out-of-time accuracy and product_family is unreliable. No resolution_log fields as inputs: they only exist after the fact. No character n-gram or SVM models: about +1 point but the reasons become unreadable and the confidence score disappears. No login, deployment or logging: the brief says a small thing that runs beats a large thing that does not. Every variant tried and dropped is in reports/experiments.md.

## 7. Anything you built or found that nobody asked for?
The finding that the labels are the bot's queue (77% right) and the three patterns behind its errors; the confidence gate and the plain-English reasons and warnings on the screen (wrong product field, "paid" but not billing); a rupee model from the policy's costs; the headcount warning (bot queue overstates Repairs 29% vs 23% and Billing 15% vs 12%); an experiments log; automated tests; the scikit-learn version pin so the saved model loads cleanly on a new machine.

## 12. What does one prediction cost, and what would a month cost at Kestrel's volume (about 700 orders a month)? Show the arithmetic.
No paid calls: one prediction costs Rs 0 in API charges (it runs locally on a normal CPU in about a millisecond, no model API or key). 700 x Rs 0 = Rs 0 a month in AI charges; hosting is the only other cost and it can run on an existing server. The business cost of using it is the human checks and the remaining errors: 700 x 20% flagged = 140 checks x Rs 305 (policy cost of a transfer) = Rs 42,700; plus 700 x 80% auto-routed x 3.4% wrong = about 19 errors x Rs 685 = Rs 13,000; total about Rs 55,700 a month (about Rs 34,000 if a check costs Rs 150).
